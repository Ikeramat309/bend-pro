# User voice synthesis

User voice answers `what hurts`, `what earns trust`, and `what language people use`. It does not establish math.

## Evidence set

- Official Apple App Store reviews for QuickBend and iBend Pipe
- Official Google Play listing and visible reviews for QuickBend
- Public discussions in r/electricians, r/IBEW, and r/conduitporn
- Public Facebook search attempted; useful post content was login-gated and excluded

Source IDs and URLs are in `SOURCE_REGISTER.md`.

## Recurring themes

### 1. Electricians want the next field action, not only the answer

Users explicitly ask for bend order, bender-facing direction, and clear step-by-step instructions. Reviews praise tools that tell them which way the bender faces and which reference to use. This supports Bend Pro's diagram-first contract: mark origin, reference, order, hook, rotate, check.

**Product rule:** Every multi-bend result must contain an ordered mark-and-bend strip. Guide content may explain why, but the Pro result must say what to do next.

### 2. Bender and conduit context are part of trust

QuickBend reviews praise choosing the brand/model, conduit type, and size. iBend Pipe feedback complains when bender/size choice is unclear. Public discussions also note that minimum physical bends and true gain change with the tool.

**Product rule:** Selected bender, EMT size, reference method, and rounding remain visible on the calculator surface. When a result does not depend on the bender, say `Bender selected for reference cues; formula uses EMT geometry` rather than implying tool-specific precision.

### 3. Fast input must still allow direct typing

A Google Play reviewer specifically disliked having to adjust values with plus/minus or sliding and wanted to type the number directly. QuickBend promotes multiple input styles.

**Product rule:** Fraction keypad/direct entry is the default. Sliders or steppers may be secondary fine-tuning tools, never the only path.

### 4. Rack and parallel work is a real differentiator

App Store reviews ask for more rack variations. Experienced electricians list matching bends and parallel kicks/offsets among the reasons they still use an app despite knowing the basic math.

**Product rule:** Rack calculators should output a full ordered table, not only a constant. Reference pipe, direction, count, and every pipe's marks belong in the result.

### 5. Terminology is dangerously ambiguous

Parallel-kick discussions repeatedly confuse side-by-side, stacked, fanned, parallel-to-cabinet, and perpendicular-to-cabinet layouts. The same thread contains mutually incompatible formulas and confident corrections.

**Product rule:** Use an illustrated geometry chooser. Labels come after the picture. Social consensus is not an acceptable formula source.

### 6. Users want speed without becoming blind to the method

Some electricians describe apps as a crutch; others with decades of experience use them daily. New apprentices report that an app can overcomplicate the basics, while experienced users value it for infrequent complex work.

**Product rule:** One result surface serves both groups:

- Pro layer: natural inputs, hero mark, action strip, warnings.
- Guide layer: measurement method, formula, center-finding, worked example, and why the result changes.

Do not force teaching content into the fast path, and do not hide the method entirely.

### 7. Multiple-bend planning has strong demand

QuickBend reviews specifically praise Multiple Bends, and public discussions ask for bend order and stick-level planning. This contradicts a simplistic `remove it` conclusion.

**Product rule:** Preserve the ambition as `Build a Run`. Import validated bend cards and show combined marks/order. Do not market a raw mark notebook as a solved multi-bend calculator.

### 8. Trust is lost when references or results appear unstable

Visible competitor feedback includes a reference-pipe control that would not change and users questioning differences from familiar charts. Public threads also show that people may not understand what an app expects or what its answer references.

**Product rule:**

- Inputs use physical nouns and show how to measure them.
- Result values state `from` and `to` origins.
- Changing any mode visibly updates the diagram and result contract.
- Reference pipe never changes implicitly.
- Formula source and assumptions are available in Guide.

## Jobs-to-be-done statements

| Workflow | Field wording to design around |
|---|---|
| Back-to-Back 90 | `I need the backs of these two 90s to land this far apart.` |
| Matching Offset | `I need the new offset to sit exactly over the one already installed.` |
| Parallel Offsets | `I need every pipe to keep the same gap through the offset.` |
| Compound 90 | `I need two 45s to wrap this exact corner shape with clearance.` |
| Build a Run | `I need all bends on this stick in the right order without running out of pipe.` |
| Parallel Kick 90s | `I need this rack to kick and turn down without the pipes closing up or missing the cabinet.` |

## Research limitations

- Storefronts surface a small and changing subset of reviews.
- Reddit participants are self-selected and may be wrong about math.
- Photos and deleted links are not always recoverable.
- Public Facebook content was not usable without authentication.

The themes are therefore product evidence, not prevalence estimates.
